3. How did RAII help manage locks?

Manual lock management is tedious and error prone, may lead to deadlocks or forgotten unlocks.

std::lock automatically acquires the lock on creation and releases it on destruction.

If an exception is thrown while the lock is held, the destructor will still be called, ensuring that the lock is released properly and preventing potential deadlocks or resource leaks.


4. How does this resemble the Map-Reduce idea?

Each thread independently processes its chunk of data, no comms between threads at this stage.

After all threads completed their work, we combine partial sums into final result.

5. What limitations does this approach have?

If there are too many threads, overhead of thread management and context switching can outweigh benefits.

Threads share memory within one process which cannot span to different machines.

You would need to process data in chunks that fit in memory.
